# PaidToBe — Ideal Customer Profiles

_Last updated: 2026-03-16_

---

## Overview

PaidToBe serves two intersecting problem spaces:
- **Job Axis:** "Is my career safe from AI? What do I do about it?"
- **Location Axis:** "Where should I live for the best safety net and quality of life?"

Five ICPs span these axes. Each enters from one side but upgrades when they discover the intersection.

---

## ICP 1: "The Displaced Creative"

### Who They Are
- **Age:** 25–40
- **Income:** $30K–$75K (formerly $50K–$120K before AI disruption)
- **Location:** US, UK, Canada — urban/suburban, often remote
- **Job type:** Freelance copywriter, graphic designer, illustrator, translator, content writer, video editor

### What They Value
Creative autonomy, craftsmanship, flexible work, self-reliance. They built a career on human creativity and now watch algorithms replicate it.

### Top Pains
1. **Income collapse is already happening.** Ramp data: companies replaced freelancers with AI at 25:1 cost savings. Freelance labor marketplace spend fell from 0.66% to 0.14% (2021–2025). One copywriter's business went from $600K/yr to under $10K.
2. **Identity crisis.** University of Florida researchers identified "AI Replacement Dysfunction" — insomnia, paranoia, loss of identity when algorithms replicate what defined your professional self.
3. **No clear pivot path.** Career coaching costs $75–$250/session. They can't afford it when income has cratered.
4. **Geographic trap.** High-cost city + declining income. Aware that Portugal has 50% lower consumer prices but overwhelmed by visa/healthcare/logistics research.
5. **Isolation.** No corporate safety net — no severance, no unemployment insurance, no employer-sponsored retraining.

### Jobs to Be Done
- **Functional:** Identify which creative skills still have market value; find a realistic career pivot; evaluate whether relocating abroad could extend their financial runway
- **Emotional:** Feel like they have a plan — reduce the paralyzing anxiety of watching income evaporate with no backup
- **Social:** Connect with others in the same situation; stop feeling alone in a career crisis

### Current Solutions (and why they fail)
| Solution | Why It Fails |
|----------|-------------|
| Reddit (r/freelance, r/antiwork) | Cathartic but actionless; no personalized guidance |
| Generic career sites (Indeed, LinkedIn) | Optimized for job seekers, not people fleeing a dying field |
| Nomad blogs / expat groups | Fragmented info, no connection to career risk data |
| ChatGPT itself | Generic responses with no labor market or country-specific data |

### Willingness to Pay
- Free: AI risk score for their job + basic country comparisons (the hook)
- $15–$29/mo: Personalized career pivot recs, relocation guides, community
- $49–$99 one-time: Deep-dive "your job + target countries" report
- $150–$300: Live advisor session for transition planning

### Entry Point
Googles "will AI replace copywriters" → lands on PaidToBe's occupation risk page → sees their displacement score → hooked because it quantifies what they've been feeling.

### Upgrade Trigger
Discovers the Location axis: "Wait, I could move to Portugal and my savings would last 3x longer while I retrain?" The career + relocation crossover is what no other tool offers.

## ICP 2: "The Anxious Professional"

### Who They Are
- **Age:** 30–50
- **Income:** $75K–$150K
- **Location:** US, Canada, UK, Australia — metro areas
- **Job type:** Mid-level corporate — marketing manager, financial analyst, project manager, HR specialist, paralegal, junior software developer

### What They Value
Stability, providing for family, career progression, competence. They've built a good life and fear losing it.

### Top Pains
1. **Ambient dread without actionable data.** 51% of workers worried about AI job loss (2026). 77% fear permanent job loss (Reuters). They read contradictory headlines daily but don't know if *their specific role* is at risk.
2. **Company won't tell the truth.** 55% of employers who laid off for AI regret it — but that doesn't help the employee who was let go. Companies announce "AI transformation" while assuring workers their jobs are safe.
3. **No Plan B.** Mortgage, kids, obligations. International relocation feels overwhelming but increasingly rational as the US safety net erodes.
4. **Skills gap anxiety.** Only 16% of workers had high "AIQ" in 2025 (Forrester). Their company offers no meaningful AI training.
5. **Decision paralysis.** Too many variables — career risk, finances, family, country options — and no integrated framework.

### Jobs to Be Done
- **Functional:** Get a credible risk assessment for their occupation; identify 2–3 upskilling paths; have a researched "Plan B" country shortlist
- **Emotional:** Replace ambient dread with informed confidence — knowing is better than not knowing
- **Social:** Explain their planning to a spouse with data, not paranoia ("here's the data, here's our backup plan")

### Current Solutions (and why they fail)
| Solution | Why It Fails |
|----------|-------------|
| News articles | Contradictory clickbait, not personalized |
| LinkedIn influencers | Survivorship bias; "just learn AI" is not a strategy |
| Career coaching ($272/hr avg) | Expensive, coaches don't understand AI displacement or relocation |
| BLS / O*NET data | Raw stats, no interpretation connecting risk to action |

### Willingness to Pay
- Free: Occupation risk score + basic country explorer
- $19–$39/mo: Monthly risk updates, curated news, adaptation roadmap
- $99–$199/yr: Full access — country deep-dives, AI advisor, prediction markets
- $200–$500: Premium "Personal Resilience Plan" report

### Entry Point
LinkedIn post about AI layoffs → Googles "AI risk score for [job title]" → finds PaidToBe. Or a friend shares their risk score on social media.

### Upgrade Trigger
The free risk score creates urgency. Upgrade happens when they realize PaidToBe connects career risk to actionable next steps (upskilling AND relocation) — something no other platform does. Often triggered by a real event: layoff round at their company, industry news, or spouse asking "what's your plan?"

## ICP 3: "The Stranded New Grad"

### Who They Are
- **Age:** 20–26
- **Income:** $0–$50K (entry-level or still job-hunting)
- **Location:** US, UK, Canada, India
- **Job type:** Seeking entry-level roles in tech, marketing, finance, design, media — the exact roles being eliminated fastest

### What They Value
Opportunity, fairness, mobility, building a life on their own terms. Willing to go anywhere if it means a real start.

### Top Pains
1. **The first rung of the career ladder is gone.** Only 30% of 2025 grads secured full-time jobs in their fields. Entry-level listings down 15%, applications up 30%. Fortune: "AI is eating the first rung."
2. **Degree may be a liability.** Higher-paying jobs requiring a bachelor's degree are *more* likely to be AI-affected. Two-thirds of parents rethinking college value.
3. **No career identity to fall back on.** Unlike mid-career pros, they don't have 15 years of relationships and transferable experience.
4. **Geographic flexibility is their one advantage — but they don't know how to use it.** Unattached enough to move internationally but overwhelmed by the research.
5. **Information overload.** "Learn to code" vs. "AI is replacing coders" vs. "learn trades" vs. "go into healthcare."

### Jobs to Be Done
- **Functional:** Identify career paths with viable entry points; determine if relocating improves their odds; build a skills roadmap accounting for AI
- **Emotional:** Feel hope — that a path forward exists, even if different from what they planned
- **Social:** Prove to parents and peers they're being strategic, not lost

### Willingness to Pay
- Price-sensitive but willing to invest (this generation pays for Skillshare, MasterClass)
- Free: Essential — hook before asking for money
- $9–$19/mo: Student pricing for career pathfinding + country rankings + community
- $49–$149 one-time: "Career Launch Plan" report

### Entry Point
TikTok/Reel: "Your degree has a 67% AI displacement score — here's what to do about it." Viral short-form content driving to PaidToBe.

### Upgrade Trigger
Discovers geographic arbitrage: "I could get a digital nomad visa in Portugal for $3,680/mo" or "Colombia only requires $750/mo." The realization that location is a strategy.

## ICP 4: "The Strategic Relocator"

### Who They Are
- **Age:** 32–55
- **Income:** $80K–$200K+
- **Location:** Currently US, UK, or Anglophone country
- **Job type:** Remote-capable professional, small business owner, or someone with dual citizenship/heritage ties (like the founder: US-based with Canadian and Slovak roots)

### What They Value
Optionality, security, family protection, rational long-term planning. They think in decades, not months.

### Top Pains
1. **The safety net gap is real and growing.** US has no UBI, weakening unemployment benefits, healthcare tied to employment. 276,000+ tech workers lost jobs in 2024–2025. "If that happens to me, what catches me?"
2. **Relocation research is fragmented.** Cross-referencing visa requirements + cost of living + healthcare + tax + safety nets + AI-economy readiness across dozens of countries requires 6+ separate tools.
3. **Citizenship/residency strategy is high-stakes.** Portugal's citizenship pathway went from 5 to 10 years. Slovakia offers EU freedom of movement but has its own requirements. Decisions made now play out over 5–10 years.
4. **Career risk informs location decisions, but no tool connects them.** They intuitively understand that "where should I live" depends on "how safe is my income" — but no platform models this.
5. **Spouse/family alignment.** Household decisions need shared data and frameworks, not one person's gut feeling.

### Jobs to Be Done
- **Functional:** Compare 5–10 countries on a unified scorecard; build a multi-year residency/citizenship timeline; integrate career risk into location planning
- **Emotional:** Feel in control of family's future rather than at the mercy of one country's trajectory
- **Social:** Be seen as prudent and forward-thinking — "I'm not fleeing, I'm diversifying"

### Current Solutions (and why they fail)
| Solution | Why It Fails |
|----------|-------------|
| Immigration lawyers ($300–$500/hr) | Expensive, one country at a time, don't understand AI labor dynamics |
| NomadList | Great for short-term nomads, weak on long-term residency/citizenship/safety nets |
| Expat Facebook groups | Anecdotal, biased toward wherever the commenter lives |
| Numbeo | One dimension of a multi-dimensional decision |

### Willingness to Pay
- **Highest WTP of any ICP.** Already spend on immigration lawyers and financial advisors.
- Free: Country comparison headlines — enough to see PaidToBe's unique value
- $39–$59/mo: Full country intelligence, policy tracking, visa planner, AI advisor
- $199–$499 one-time: Personalized Relocation Intelligence Report
- $500–$2,000: Premium concierge — human advisor + AI for complex multi-country strategies

### Entry Point
Googles "best countries to move to from the US 2026" or "countries with best safety net for expats." Finds PaidToBe's scoring system through SEO.

### Upgrade Trigger
The free comparison reveals something they didn't know — a country they hadn't considered ranks higher, or a visa pathway they thought was closed is actually open. The "aha" of career risk + country readiness in one view is what makes them pay. Accelerates when policy changes and PaidToBe alerts them first.

## ICP 5: "The Concerned Parent"

### Who They Are
- **Age:** 38–55
- **Income:** $80K–$180K household
- **Location:** US, Canada, UK, Australia — suburban, family-oriented
- **Job type:** Employed professional; primarily motivated by children's futures, not their own job risk

### What They Value
Family security, education, giving children the best start, pragmatism.

### Top Pains
1. **Parenting in an unprecedented transition.** 53% of parents concerned AI will narrow children's job prospects. 62% discussed it with their teens in the past two weeks.
2. **Education system is lagging.** Two-thirds of parents say AI is forcing them to rethink a four-year college. They're about to spend $100K–$300K on education with no confidence it's the right investment.
3. **"What should my kid study?" has no good answer.** Traditional guidance (counselor → college → career) was built for a stable labor market.
4. **Dual anxiety.** Their own job is shifting WHILE they worry about what their children will do.
5. **Relocation as a family strategy.** Some are seriously considering whether universal healthcare, free university, and stronger safety nets in another country would better serve their family.

### Jobs to Be Done
- **Functional:** Understand which career fields have the strongest 10–20 year outlook; evaluate international education/residency for children; assess own career risk as part of family planning
- **Emotional:** Feel like a responsible parent making data-informed decisions
- **Social:** Have credible data to share with spouse and family

### Willingness to Pay
- Motivated spenders for children's futures (already pay for tutors, test prep, college counseling)
- Free: "See the AI risk score for careers your teen is considering"
- $19–$39/mo: Family membership — track multiple career fields, education pathways, country comparisons
- $99–$299 one-time: "Family Future Plan" report
- $500+: Premium family advisory session

### Entry Point
Article: "The career your kid is studying for has a 71% AI displacement score" or "These 5 countries offer free university + the strongest safety nets." Parent-focused content marketing.

### Upgrade Trigger
PaidToBe answers a question no one else can: "Given what AI is doing to jobs, should my family stay here or would we be better off somewhere else — and what should my kids study either way?"
